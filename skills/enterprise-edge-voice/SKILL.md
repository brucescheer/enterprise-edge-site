# The Enterprise Edge voice

Three people run this programme. If the three of us generate content
separately, we produce three brands and a reader has to work out we are the
same thing. This file exists so that does not happen.

## Rule one: the website is the source of truth

**getenterpriseedge.com is canonical. Read it before you write.**

If something you are drafting disagrees with the site, the site wins, or the
site gets changed first and then the asset follows. Never publish an asset
that contradicts it. When in doubt, open the page and quote it.

That applies to dates, venues, times, prices, seat counts, the names of the
three edges, and every line of the spine below.

## The narrative spine

The site follows Bruce's book: name the **Problem**, show the **Outcome**,
give the **Path**, prescribe the **Steps**. Long-form assets should run in
that order. Short ones can compress, but they must not open on us. Open on
the reader's problem. We come fourth.

Use these words. They are the site's, verbatim.

**Problem.** Upmarket revenue misfires.
> Enterprise revenue depends on three connected motions: win preference,
> earn approval, and prove value after the sale. They fail in completely
> different ways, and most revenue organizations are strong at one,
> adequate at the second, and quietly losing money on the third. The trouble
> is that the one you are worst at never sends you a report. The reason is
> usually somewhere inside the customer organization, in a meeting you were
> not in.

**Outcome.** High performing revenue engine.
> Your champion makes the same value case you would have made, in their own
> words, without you there to correct it. Procurement can defend the
> commercial logic. Finance understands the economics. After the sale the
> customer can see what was delivered and where more value sits.

**Path.** Get the Enterprise Edge.

**Steps.** Take the assessment. Check the dates. Register. Ask us anything.

## The three edges, and the three promises

Never rename these. The Edge is the product name; the promise is the line
underneath it. Both are fixed.

| | Edge | Owner | Promise |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | The Value Edge | Bruce Scheer | Get them to say yes |
| 02 | The Approval Edge | Dean Edwards | Get the deal approved |
| 03 | The Expansion Edge | Sandy Yu | Get them to expand |

The headline they come from:
**Get them to say yes. Get the damn deal approved. Get them to expand.**

Three different problems. One connected motion. Not a journey, not a flow,
not a funnel.

## The programme

**The Enterprise Edge Masterclass.** Not a roadshow, not a workshop series.
Half a day, three hours plus the meal, five cities: San Francisco, Los
Angeles, Singapore, Dallas, Austin. Twenty-five seats a city, approved in
advance. US$99 a seat and S$99 in Singapore.

**The fee is a token fee.** It does not cover the room or the meal and it is
not meant to. It exists to protect the show rate, because a seat that cost
something is a seat that gets used. Never write copy claiming it covers
costs.

**Who it is for.** B2B revenue and customer success leaders running 50
sellers or more, typically above $100 million in revenue. Say so early. A
reader who does not qualify should be able to stop reading, and one who does
should recognise themselves immediately.

## Hard rules

**No dashes.** No em dash, no en dash, and no hyphen doing the work of one.
Recast with a period or a colon. The em dash is the loudest AI tell in
published writing and this is the one rule with no exceptions.

**Nobody carries a case.** Champions **make** the case and **take** it into
the room. Same for a narrative or a story. Never "carry".

**Every statistic is sourced, named inline, and linked.** Open the source,
find the number on the page, then publish it. If it cannot be traced, cut
it. A confident number with no source behind it is the fastest way to lose
a room full of people who run vendor reviews for a living.

**We show up as one thing.** The commercial arrangement between the three of
us is a fact for a contract, not a message for an asset. Never write that
you can hire one of us separately, that we contract individually, or that
there is no legal entity. Never give one of the three a link or a credential
the other two do not get.

**No Bowtie, hourglass, or Winning by Design stage vocabulary.** The three
edges cover the same ground and that model belongs to someone else.

**Banned words.** Journey (except buyer's journey or hero's journey), delve,
landscape, unlock, leverage as a verb, robust, seamless, elevate, empower,
navigate as a metaphor, game-changer, synergy, holistic, cutting-edge,
transformative, crucial, pivotal, foster, dive in, "it's worth noting", "in
today's fast-paced world".

## How it should sound

Peer to peer with revenue leaders, never lecturing. Second person. Short
paragraphs, one to four sentences. A longer explanatory sentence, then a
short declarative hit. Colons for definitions and reveals. Plain words.
Read it aloud: it should sound like one of us on a stage, not a whitepaper.

The buyer is the hero. We are the enabler. If a draft makes the programme
or the three of us the hero, it has failed.

Write scenes, not abstractions. "The champion who went quiet" beats "a
stalled opportunity". "The one you are worst at never sends you a report"
beats "visibility challenges".

## Before you publish, check

- Does it open on the reader's problem rather than on us?
- Does every fact match getenterpriseedge.com?
- Are the three edges named correctly, with the right promise under each?
- Zero dashes?
- Every statistic sourced and linked?
- Nobody carrying anything?
- Does it read as one programme rather than three consultants?
- Would a CRO with 50 sellers know in the first two lines that this is for
  them?
